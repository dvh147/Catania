# Chapter 4: Day by Day — Your Week

*14–20 March 2026*

---

> *This is a suggested itinerary, not a contract. Things will go sideways.
> The toddler will have opinions. It will rain on a day you planned for the
> beach. A restaurant will be closed for a cousin's wedding. Embrace all of
> it. The best travel moments are usually the unplanned ones — and Sicily
> is very good at producing them.*

## The Setup

**You arrive:** Saturday 14 March, evening.
**You leave:** Friday 20 March, at the airport by ~1pm (allow for a
leisurely 12:30pm departure from the apartment; the airport is 10 minutes
away by car from your address).

**Your apartment:** Via Cristoforo Colombo 20, 95121 Catania. This puts
you in the waterfront district, between the historic centre and the port —
a practical position. The Piazza del Duomo is about 15–20 minutes on foot
heading north. The airport is about 10 minutes by car heading south. The
seafront and the main coastal road north toward Aci Trezza are right there.
Good for day trips in all directions; not quite in the baroque centre's
paving, which makes for a quieter sleep.

**Your actual days:**

| Date | Day | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Sat 14 Mar | Day 1 | Arrive evening |
| Sun 15 Mar | Day 2 | Full day |
| Mon 16 Mar | Day 3 | Full day |
| Tue 17 Mar | Day 4 | Full day |
| Wed 18 Mar | Day 5 | Full day |
| Thu 19 Mar | Day 6 | Full day |

Five full days. One arrival evening. One departure morning. Enough for

## The Weather Picture

*Based on available forecast data for Catania, 14–20 March 2026:*

The week looks mild and generally decent. Temperatures hovering around
**18°C during the day, 11°C at night** — comfortable for walking, cool
enough in the evenings for a layer. Wind is present throughout (12–16 km/h
westerly, more on some days) — noticeable but not a problem at sea level.
March averages about 10 rain days over the month, meaning you should expect
some rain during the week, but not persistent rain across multiple days.

**The strategic implication:** Etna (the day most dependent on clear skies)
is placed on Monday, early in the week, to maximise the chance of catching
a clear morning. If Monday is cloudy at altitude, Tuesday is the fallback.
The rest of the itinerary is broadly rain-flexible.

**Check the forecast the evening before each day trip.** For Etna
specifically, check the mountain-level forecast (not just Catania city
level) — the summit has its own weather system. [AccuWeather](https://www.accuweather.com/en/it/catania/215605/weather-forecast/215605) and
[Meteo.it](https://www.meteo.it) are both reliable for local conditions.

## Structural notes before we begin

**Toddler nap logic** is built into every day. In practice, this means
protecting the midday window (roughly 12:30–3pm) for something stationary —
a long lunch, a café, the apartment — so the child sleeps and the adults sit
still and drink wine without guilt. The days are not packed. There is
breathing room. This is intentional.

**The car** stays parked from Sunday through Thursday's day trips. You walk
from the apartment to the historic centre on city days (15–20 minutes, flat,
along the waterfront and up). On day trip days, the car leaves early from
your street and comes back by late afternoon.

**The format for each day:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Morning** | Best hours — cooler, energetic, less crowded |
| **Midday** | Nap window — strategic stillness |
| **Afternoon** | Second wind — often the best sightseeing slot |
| **Evening** | Passeggiata, aperitivo, dinner at Sicilian time |
| **Ratings** | Stroller friendliness / Budget / Exertion level |

## Saturday 14 March — Arrive & Find Your Feet

*Mood: slow, orientation, arrival. Do not attempt anything ambitious.*

You have driven from the airport with luggage and a toddler who has been
on a plane. Via Cristoforo Colombo 20 is ten minutes from the airport by
car. This is one of the most civilised airport-to-apartment journeys you
will ever make. Take advantage of it.

### Evening
Check in. Drop the bags. Park the car in your street or nearby — you will
not need it tomorrow (see Chapter 3 for ZTL and parking notes). Let the
apartment happen.

Then: if the toddler has survived the journey with any energy remaining,
walk north for 15 minutes toward **Piazza del Duomo**. The evening light on
the cathedral and the elephant fountain, with the baroque facades lit and
the passeggiata in full swing, is a very good introduction to Catania.
Not a serious sightseeing objective — just a first look. Stand at the
fountain. Note the elephant. File it for tomorrow.

Find a restaurant within easy walking distance of the apartment for dinner —
something simple, close, unheroic. Pizza is a legitimate first night choice
and Sicily does it well. Go to bed early. Tomorrow is Sunday and the
fishmarket does not wait.

> **On the apartment's position:** Via Cristoforo Colombo runs along
> the waterfront south of the historic centre. You are close to the port,
> which means the sounds of the sea and the distant clatter of the docks are
> part of your mornings. You are also very close to the coastal road north —
> which is the direction of Aci Trezza. The historic centre is a pleasant
> 15-minute walk; the airport, when Friday comes, is ten minutes by car. The
> location is practical in all directions.

| Stroller | Budget | Exertion |
|---|---|---|
| ★★★★★ Evening walk on flat waterfront streets | € — One meal, nothing else | Minimal — this is an arrival day |

## Sunday 15 March — Deep Catania

*Mood: city explorer. This is the day Catania earns its place in the guide.*

### Morning — The Market (6:30am. Set the alarm.)

**La Pescheria** does not wait and does not care that it's Sunday. The market
runs seven days a week and the Sunday morning has, if anything, a slightly
more local and relaxed feel — fewer commercial buyers, more families, more
people who are there because they love it rather than because they need
wholesale fish by 8am.

Walk north from the apartment (15 minutes) and arrive at the market before
9am. See Chapter 2 for the full account of what you'll find. The short version:
extraordinary, vivid, loud, wet, unmissable. Use the carrier for the toddler
inside the market. Eat something from the vendors at the edge — boiled octopus
or fried things in paper, before breakfast, because you're in Catania now.

From La Pescheria, walk north on Via Etnea: the Collegiata facade (look up,
look at the black and gold), the entrance to **Via dei Crociferi** (duck down
this side street — it looks like a film set and is), the facade of the
**Benedictine Monastery** if you haven't booked a tour.

**If the weather is fine this morning:** Sunday is a good day to be out in
the centre — the city has a slightly different rhythm, with families and
the post-church passeggiata. The piazza will be busy in a good way.

### Midday
Lunch near **Piazza Bellini** — near the Roman theatre, with a largely
university-student clientele and accordingly honest pricing. Order fresh
fish if the market morning put you in the mood, or pasta alla Norma, the
city's own dish. This is the right moment for it.

Nap window: back to the apartment or a long café stop.

### Afternoon
Choose based on morning energy:

**Option A:** The **Roman Theatre** — an excavation of ancient Rome built
under a residential neighbourhood. Twenty minutes inside is enough to be
moved by it; more if the adults want to stay. Combine with the **Amphitheatre
at Piazza Stesicoro** — 10 minutes' walk — where a Roman arena sits under
a traffic roundabout and you look down through a fence into the first century.

**Option B:** If the morning was full: **Castello Ursino** (15 minutes south
of the cathedral). The Norman castle that used to be on the coast until
a lava flow moved the coastline 800 metres. Exterior always worth it;
museum inside depending on nap status.

### Evening
Passeggiata on Via Etnea, south toward the Duomo, at golden hour. The cathedral
in late afternoon light. Gelato. Watch the social theatre of a Catanian
Sunday evening — it is a properly Italian institution and this is one of
the best places to observe it.

Dinner in the Borgo area (north of centre — 20-minute walk, but worth it
for the local rather than tourist-facing atmosphere) or near the apartment
if the day has been long enough.

> **Rainy version:** La Pescheria works in rain (partially covered, the
> light is actually better). Replace the afternoon outdoor options with the
> Benedictine Monastery tour (book ahead) or Castello Ursino museum. A long
> lunch is not a failure — in Sicily it is the point.

| Stroller | Budget | Exertion |
|---|---|---|
| ★★★☆☆ Mixed — carrier for market, side streets uneven | €€ — some entrance fees, lunch out | Medium — a lot of walking, all flat |

## Monday 16 March — Mt Etna

*Mood: adventure, altitude, the volcano you've been looking at all weekend.*

**Check the weather Sunday night.** Not just Catania — the mountain-level
forecast. Etna in cloud is not Etna. If the forecast shows low cloud or
rain at altitude on Monday, move this day to Tuesday and do the coast
day instead. A clear morning is non-negotiable.

*Based on current forecast data: Monday looks mild and generally clear at
city level. Mountain conditions will need confirming closer to the date.*

### Morning (leave the apartment by 7:30am)
Drive south toward the airport, past the airport, and then inland — the
road through **Nicolosi** to **Rifugio Sapienza** on the south slope.
45 minutes from Via Cristoforo Colombo (the airport proximity helps — you
are already pointing in the right direction).

The cable car, the jeep to 2,900m, the sulphur smell, the lava landscape,
the view over the whole of eastern Sicily below — see Chapter 5 for the
full account. The short version: dress in warm layers, put the toddler in
the carrier above the cable car station, and allow the scale of the place
to do its work.

Aim to be back down the mountain (cable car descent) by noon.

### Midday
Lunch at **Zafferana Etnea** on the way down — honey capital of the Etna
slopes, good local trattorias, more interesting than eating at the cable
car base station. Nap in the car on the drive back.

### Afternoon
Back at the apartment by 2:30–3pm. This is deliberately easy — after Etna,
low-key is right. A walk along the waterfront from the apartment. Gelato.
Nothing that requires thought.

### Evening
Dinner that acknowledges the day — you went up a volcano with a toddler
and came back for dinner. Something unhurried and good near home base.

> **If Monday is cloud-covered on the mountain:** swap Monday and Tuesday
> (coast day Monday, Etna attempt Tuesday). The coast day is entirely
> weather-flexible; Etna is not.

| Stroller | Budget | Exertion |
|---|---|---|
| ★★☆☆☆ Carrier essential above cable car; pushchair stays at base | €€€ — cable car + jeep + lunch | High — altitude, cold, terrain |

## Tuesday 17 March — The Coast

*Mood: relaxed, salty, restorative.*

### Morning
Drive north from the apartment along the coastal road — the same direction
as Via Cristoforo Colombo runs — to **Aci Trezza**. About 20 minutes,
no motorway, no toll. The sea is immediately to your right for most of it.

**Aci Trezza:** the fishing village, the harbour, the **Faraglioni** — the
basalt sea stacks that Polyphemus hurled at Odysseus. Walk the waterfront,
look at the rocks, get a coffee, let the child run at the sea. If the
water is calm, the 30-minute boat trip around the sea stacks is a real
hit with toddlers. If not, the view from shore is extraordinary anyway.

From Aci Trezza, **Aci Castello** is 10 minutes south along the coast road
(or a 20-minute waterfront walk) — the Norman castle on its basalt rock
platform over the sea. Exterior for sure; museum inside if it takes your fancy.

**Note on beach:** the coast here is volcanic rock platforms and dark volcanic
sand rather than soft beach. Excellent for rockpool paddling; not ideal for
sandcastle-building. For sandy beach: Lido di Plaia (south of Catania,
past the airport, 15 minutes from the apartment) is the alternative.

### Midday
Lunch at a waterfront trattoria in Aci Trezza. Fresh fish. The catch was
in the water this morning. Order the swordfish or the pasta with clams.
House white wine, cold.

Nap window along the waterfront promenade, or at a quiet café table.

### Afternoon
Rock platforms and sea, or the beach if you decide to drive south to
Lido di Plaia. Back toward the apartment by 5pm.

### Evening
An easy evening — sea air does the work. Aperitivo, simple dinner near home.

> **Rain version:** Aci Castello in light rain is dramatic. Heavy rain:
> skip the coast and drive to **Acireale** instead (10 minutes past Aci
> Trezza) — a small baroque city on the clifftop with a fine piazza and
> excellent pasticcerie.

| Stroller | Budget | Exertion |
|---|---|---|
| ★★★★☆ Good along the waterfront; rock platforms need a carrier | € — Cheaper eating, free beach areas | Low — this is the rest day |

## Wednesday 18 March — Taormina

*Mood: spectacular. 400 photographs minimum.*

### Morning (leave by 7:30am)
A18 motorway north — 50 minutes from Catania. Park at **Mazzarò** (lower
car parks at the base of the cliff), take the **funivia** cable car up.
Do not drive into Taormina itself.

First priority at the top: the **Teatro Greco-Romano**. Go before anything
else. The theatre opens at 9am; the view from the upper tiers — stage arch
framing the sea, Etna filling the northern horizon — is the moment that
justifies the whole day trip. Get there before the tour buses arrive at
10am. See Chapter 6 for full detail.

After the theatre: **Piazza IX Aprile** (the great terrace viewpoint, the
Corso, do not miss it), **Villa Comunale** (the English-designed gardens
at the east end, sea views, space for the toddler to run).

**Wednesday in March** is a good Taormina day — mid-week, pre-season, the
town is navigable. Light rain is not a reason to cancel; the theatre in
mist is a different kind of beautiful.

### Midday
Lunch just off the Corso Umberto — one alley back from the main drag, where
the prices and quality both improve. Piazza IX Aprile café for at least one
drink with the view.

Nap: a bench in the Villa Comunale works well.

### Afternoon
Explore the back streets if energy allows. Ceramics shopping for the things
you've been planning to buy all week. Drive back by 4:30pm.

### Evening
Quiet. Taormina days are full days. Aperitivo, simple dinner near the apartment.

> **Rain version:** Light rain — go, bring umbrellas. Heavy rain — swap with
> the Benedictine Monastery day in Catania and push Taormina to Thursday.

| Stroller | Budget | Exertion |
|---|---|---|
| ★★☆☆☆ Carrier needed for theatre; Corso manageable | €€€ — parking + funivia + tourist-town lunch | Medium — a lot of walking on clifftop terrain |

## Thursday 19 March — Syracuse & Ortigia

*Mood: the big one. The most culturally rich day of the week. Give it
the space it deserves — this is your last full day.*

### Morning (leave by 8am)
A19 motorway south — 1 hour from Catania. The road passes close to the
airport direction before heading inland; from Via Cristoforo Colombo you
are already pointing the right way and the initial few kilometres are on
familiar ground.

The **Neapolis Archaeological Park**: the Greek theatre (5th century BC,
still enormous, still moving), the Roman amphitheatre immediately adjacent
(the contrast between what the Greeks and Romans chose to build for public
entertainment is worth contemplating), the **Ear of Dionysius** (the cave
Caravaggio named, where you whisper to the far wall and it whispers back).
Allow 2–3 hours. See Chapter 7 for the full account.

### Midday
Cross to **Ortigia** — 10 minutes by car or a 20-minute walk. Lunch in
the streets behind the main waterfront (not the obvious tourist restaurants
on the main piazza). The Ortigia market on Via Trento may still be running
if you arrive before noon — worth a quick pass for street food and the
atmosphere.

Nap on the Ortigia waterfront — benches facing the sea, the pram doing its
work, the adults sitting with something cold and looking at the harbour.
One of the better nap-window locations of the week.

### Afternoon
Walk Ortigia without a plan. The **Cathedral** built into a Greek temple
(the Doric columns are still there, still load-bearing, inside the baroque
church — see them). The **Fountain of Arethusa** at the southern tip of
the island (the freshwater spring, the papyrus plants, the Greek myth
made physical). The **waterfront** for aperitivo as the light changes
over the sea.

This is your last night in Sicily in terms of going anywhere. Make the
aperitivo a proper one.

### Evening
Drive back to Catania by early evening (1 hour). The farewell dinner — save
this for tonight. Find somewhere you've identified during the week as the
right place for the occasion. A full Sicilian meal, from antipasto to dolce.
A bottle of something good — Etna Rosso if you're drinking red, Etna Bianco
if white. Cannoli for dessert, filled to order.

Then the passeggiata. One last drift along Via Etnea. The volcano in the
dark. Go to bed late. It's your last proper night.

> **Rain version:** Ortigia in the rain is beautiful — the pale limestone
> streets, the cathedral, the covered market. Come anyway. For the Neapolis
> park in heavy rain: replace with the **Paolo Orsi Archaeological Museum**
> (one of the best in Europe, entirely indoors, immediately adjacent to the
> park entrance).

| Stroller | Budget | Exertion |
|---|---|---|
| ★★★☆☆ Ortigia excellent; park gravel paths harder | €€ — entrance fees + lunch + farewell dinner | Medium-high — a long day, worth every step |

## Friday 20 March — The Morning Before the Airport

*Mood: savouring without sentimentality. You have until 12:30pm.*

You need to be at the airport by 1pm. The airport is 10 minutes from the
apartment by car. So: leave the apartment no later than 12:30pm with bags
in the car.

That gives you the morning — roughly 8am to 12pm — for a last few hours
in the city you now know.

### The Last Morning (8am–noon)

**Option A — Market one last time:**
Walk to La Pescheria (15 minutes). The market at 8am on a Friday is the
market at full power. Go back. You'll see things differently the second time —
or fifth time — having spent the week building context. Buy something from
a vendor. Eat something standing up. Then the **Mercato di Via Plebiscito**
for last-minute food shopping: blood oranges by the kilo (buy aggressively
— they barely survive the journey but the ones that do are extraordinary),
pistachio cream, dried oregano, whatever you missed earlier in the week.

**Option B — Slow bar breakfast:**
Find the bar you've come to love over the week. Order everything on the
pastry shelf. Take your time. Let the toddler eat their body weight in
cornetti. Read nothing. Look at the street.

**Either way:** Back to the apartment by 11am. Pack. Load the car. One
last check of the rooms (the charger behind the bathroom mirror, the
sunglasses on the kitchen table). Drive to the airport.

The airport at Fontanarossa is straightforward to navigate. Return the car,
check in, eat a last arancino at the terminal. It will not be as good as
the ones from the market. Order it anyway.

> **What not to do this morning:** attempt anything that requires more than
> 20 minutes of engagement. This is not the morning for the Benedictine
> Monastery tour. This is the morning for buying blood oranges and sitting
> with a coffee.

| Stroller | Budget | Exertion |
|---|---|---|
| ★★★★★ Familiar streets, familiar rhythm | € — Market shopping and one last breakfast | Low — the morning is for being, not doing |
